FAQ

Questions Winnipeg homeowners ask us.

Costs vary by material, height, and total length — a typical residential wood or chain link fence often runs $30–$70 per linear foot installed, with vinyl and commercial fencing running higher. You’ll get an exact, no-obligation quote after a quick property review.

Most standard-height residential fences don’t require a building permit, but the City of Winnipeg does regulate maximum height by yard (front, side, and rear) and restricts fence height near corner lots and driveways for sightline safety. Your matched pro can confirm what applies to your property before starting.

Winnipeg’s heavy clay soil expands and contracts hard through winter freeze-thaw cycles. Posts that aren’t set deep enough below the frost line — or aren’t set in enough concrete — can heave, lean, or pop over a few winters. It’s the single biggest cause of fence failure in this city.

There’s no automatic 50/50 rule — it comes down to what you and your neighbour agree to. Many homeowners split the cost of a shared boundary fence, but it’s worth confirming the property line with a survey or existing pins before building.

A typical residential yard is usually finished in 1–3 days once posts are set, though concrete needs time to cure before panels go up. Commercial and longer runs take longer — your quote will include a realistic timeline.

Often, yes — leaning posts, broken panels, and sagging gates can frequently be repaired for a fraction of full replacement cost. Mention repair when requesting your quote and your matched pro will assess what’s salvageable.
